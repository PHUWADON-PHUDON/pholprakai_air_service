Narration master

How did one of history's greatest kingdoms lose its power? It did not collapse in a single catastrophe. Internal divisions weakened royal control, while powerful neighbors pressed closer. In the seventh century BCE, Assyrian armies invaded Egypt. They captured Memphis, exposing the limits of Egyptian resistance. In 525 BCE, Persia conquered Egypt, though independence later returned. Native dynasties regained the throne, but another Persian conquest followed. Alexander arrived in 332 BCE, replacing Persian rule with Macedonian power. The Ptolemies ruled next, blending Greek court culture with Egyptian kingship. Egypt remained powerful, but its rulers depended increasingly on Mediterranean politics. Cleopatra's kingdom became entangled in Rome's struggle for power. After Actium in 31 BCE, their position became untenable. In 30 BCE, Rome annexed Egypt and ended the Ptolemaic kingdom. But Egyptian traditions, temples, and religious life continued under Roman rule. Egypt did not disappear. Its independence did.
